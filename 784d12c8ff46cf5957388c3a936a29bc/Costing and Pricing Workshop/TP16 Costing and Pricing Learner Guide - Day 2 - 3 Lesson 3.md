LESSON THREE:

PRICING LEVELS EXPLAINED

We have spoken about pricing methodologies and the various information you need to consider when determining your
selling price. However, it is very seldom that you will sell to one type of customer only, so you will need to set different
levels of pricing to service the different customer types you may engage with.

Can you think of a few of the different types of customers you may encounter, and how their needs may differ from each other?

________________________________________

Remember, a key secret to being successful in sales is to make it as easy as possible for your client to do business with
you – and this starts with having a clear pricing strategy. As your business grows into new markets this can become quite
complicated, and so a clear understanding of some basics is essential if you are to be a confident and successful salesperson.

If you sell via different channels, you need to ensure you have the necessary pricing in place to service all of them. These
channels could include one or more of the following:

•  Distributor pricing

•  Wholesale pricing

•  Retail pricing

•  Corporate/Custom pricing

•  Agent pricing

We are going to look at these various levels, explain how they differ and see how and where they fit in to your sales
strategy:

1.  Distributor Pricing would be the lowest you are prepared to go, and reserved for your very largest customers, those
who order in volume and sell on to other shops and retailers in their markets. In order to qualify for this very low
pricing, distributors should:

•  Be obliged to order in bulk (e.g. 200 of an item, minimum order value of R50,000)

•  Sell on to other retailers, and be able to provide proof of their retail network

•  Have set performance targets per quarter/year to maintain that pricing

•  Have a contractual agreement with you highlighting all roles & responsibilities on both sides

service-based business when selling bulk services to a middleman – such as bed nights at a guesthouse to a travel
agency, construction services to an architect or training for delegates to a conference organiser.

As with Distributor pricing, Wholesale pricing can have some conditions attached from your side as the supplier, e.g:

•  Only customers buying for the purpose of resale can get wholesale prices

•  Wholesale customers are obliged to stick to set sales terms & conditions and minimum quantities per product to

qualify for wholesale pricing

•  You may ask a potential customer to provide proof of their credentials (e.g. where is the shop, other suppliers

they deal with, references etc)

3.

 Retail Pricing is when you sell directly to the end user. It should be pitched high (on a par with what other shops sell
your products for). This is important because if the retail outlets you supply at a Wholesale price (and who have to
buy in quantity to get that price) find out you are selling to the general public at the same price, they will no longer
support you.

Retail pricing is what you would charge when engaging in any form of direct selling, whether at a market, trade
show, via a website/e-commerce or shop/workspace. It would also apply when selling a service directly to the end
user, such as a game drive to a tourist who books directly or a training course directly to a delegate.

4.

 Corporate/Custom  Pricing  would  involve  prices  quoted  when  the  intention  of  the  buyer  is  to  use  the  items  for
promotional purposes i.e. not to mark up and resell, or when you are designing or creating a special, customized
product  or  service  for  a  certain  client,  such  as  a  branded  item  with  their  company  logo  on  it  or  a  bespoke  team-
building session that matches the company culture & focus.

This Corporate price will usually be pitched somewhere between your wholesale and retail price, both to cover the
development costs associated with customized work, and also because in most instances the buyer does not need to
make a profit from these products or services and has a different agenda.  In a service context, this pricing would
apply when you customize a service especially for a client i.e. it is outside of your standard offerings.

Note  that  if  you  are  creating  a  once-off,  bespoke  offering,  whether  product  or  service,  you  may  actually  need  to
charge even more than you would for retail to cover your development time – depends on context.

5.

 Agent Pricing would be pricing you would give to a prospective agent,   someone who will represent you in a certain
market  or  geographical  area  and  sell  on  a  commission  basis.  As  a  benchmark,  your  wholesale  price  plus  15%
commission would be an appropriate Agent price – the agent would then quote this price to the customer, who would
pay you directly and you would then pay over the 15% commission to the agent at an agreed date.

If you follow the guidelines above, do your research and talk to your customers, you will be empowered and feel confident
that the price you quote, is the correct one.
