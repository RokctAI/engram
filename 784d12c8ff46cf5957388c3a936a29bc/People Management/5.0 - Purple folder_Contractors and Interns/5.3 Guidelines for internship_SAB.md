**What is an internship?**

An internship is an opportunity for a graduate to bridge the gap between what was learnt in the education system, and what is needed in business. It is also an opportunity to apply some of the knowledge and skills he/she has acquired during academic learning in the work place.

A good internship program includes experiential learning, and provides value to host business and intern alike. Internship should allow students to develop their skills and gain experience, whilst mirroring the real work environment. Internships vary - some can be done on a paid agreement, some for academic credits (coordinated through your department), whilst others are done on a volunteering basis.

**Forms of internship**

There are two basic types of internship – accredited (formal) and non-accredited (informal). Accredited internships are overseen by the academic body providing the accreditation (eg. University, FET College or SETA) and follow strict systems and processes designed to ensure that each intern completes specific tasks needed to gain accreditation. The focus is generally on the learner rather than the host business, and one criticism of this form of internship is that they tend to create a false impression of what work really is.

A non-accredited (Informal) internship is one that is developed and run by the host business or organising body. Tasks and assignments are entirely practical and value-adding to the host business, providing a real-life pathway for learning on the job. Criticism of this form of internship is that the learning experience can be quite challenging, and does not follow the same pathway for all interns. The upside is that interns have a very ‘real’ work experience and feel the pleasure and satisfaction of creating value in an organisation.

Internships can be paid or Voluntary. Intern payments are called Stipends.

**Pros and Cons of hiring an intern:**

The Pro’s:

  - Discover potential future employees: For most SMEs, the process of recruiting and hiring new employees drains the company\`s resources. When it comes to hiring, hosting an intern is the best way to do it. This is because by hosting an intern it gives you, as an employer, enough time and opportunity to make sure you have chosen the right person to work for your company before you consider employing them on a full-time basis.

> By hosting an intern for 3 or 6 months, you will be able to see during that period whether he/she has the potential to get the job done, and whether he/she is a good fit with the rest of the team.

  - Increase of productivity: A graduate intern provides an extra set of capable, educated hands. Not only does this allow you to expand the scope of what your company offers, but good interns also help the current staff by reducing their workload. This assists the rest of the team to concentrate on more pressing matters that require their particular expertise. The result is increased productivity within the business.

  - Fresh ideas: Graduates recently out of university are able to bring valuable and innovative ideas and skills to the host business, such as in social media and other rapidly-changing business variables. With a fresh viewpoint, interns might also identify issues that are having a negative effect on the business, for instance policies and procedures that are hampering team performance.

  - Inexpensive skills: The salary for an internship is considerably lower than that of full time employment. Furthermore, employers are not obligated to hire the intern on a full time basis should the intern fail to deliver as expected. Despite the fact that interns are not paid a lot, generally they are, almost without exception, motivated and willing to work hard to prove themselves.

  - Use of technology: Information Communication Technology (ICT) has a major impact on how people run their business. Most young adults are familiar with the latest technology, and most importantly they are able to make use of it.

> By hosting interns in your business, chances are high that your business might benefit from their knowledge, particularly where the use of technology is concerned.

  - High levels of employment contribute to the development of the country: Nearly one million South African graduates are unemployed. Unemployment has negative effects on the country and this is bad for business as well. An internship not only offers benefits to the host business, but also helps the intern become a productive member of society who can contribute to the development of the country.

The Cons

  - Time-Intensive: Hosting an intern can, and often does, involve mentoring and training the graduate to ensure that both parties get full benefit. For interns, an internship is a learning process where they can gain additional skills and valuable working experience, but this often means that the employer will have to sacrifice some of their time as part of the mentorship and training process.

  - Need for clear goals for the internship program: For the internship to be valuable and significant there has to be clear goals that are in place. This will also give some structure and objectives to the internship program. Therefore, as an employer you should not try to substitute full time employees for interns.

  - Tempered expectations: A fresh-faced intern just out of university will very seldom be the finished article. If you are expecting an intern to handle the same jobs and workload from the outset as a highly experienced person who has worked in the industry for years, you may well end up disappointed.

  - Horses for courses: It is essential to think carefully about the kind of work you give to interns. If you limit your interns and give them mundane tasks, it will probably cause resentment and low productivity, but if you over-extend them and expect miracles, this could lead to burn-out. A good plan of manageable, supervised tasks is initially a great place to start.

**How to find interns?**

  - Approach tertiary institutions- Advertise to their students

  - Put out a call for interns specifying the role and requirements- This will attract interested parties

  - Approach youth / community organisations

**What to look out for?**

When recruiting an intern into your business there are a few things to consider:

  - Review CV and highlight Red Flags

  - Carefully go through the candidate’s CV and where unsure, mark red flags you wold like to address.

  - This process helps with identifying important information which may not be clear on the CV.

Send out a mini assignment/task before inviting the candidate to an interview:

  - To avoid time wasters, send a potential candidate a written task/ research assignment.

  - Skills test-On the job task to complete

  - If they’re any good they’re worth pursuing\!

Background Checks: We recommend that recruiters complete background checks on graduates before making placements. Whilst this is especially important for candidates seeking a position that requires high security or a position of trust, such as in a home, school, hospital, financial institution, airport, and the like, background checking should be a standard part of your recruitment process.

Typically checks include verification of past employment, and reference checks, qualifications, credit history, and criminal history.

There is a huge amount of CV fraud in South Africa and we have personally seen trumped up CVs from youngsters claiming to have work experience when in fact they were in jail on murder charges at the time\! Fake degrees, fudged grades and false work-experience claims are common.

Social Media: Check out their current activity on Social Media this says a lot about the type of person you want to employ.

  - Conduct Interviews/ Personality/skills test:

  - Do the intern’s values align with your own?

  - How would he/she react in scenarios? (best/worst case)

  - Does he/she possess the right qualities/skills needed in your organisation?

  - How does he /she think? Logical?

  - How does he/she present themselves? - Professional? Sloppy?
