**4.2. INTERVIEW QUESTIONS**

### BELOW IS A LIST OF QUESTIONS THAT YOU CAN ASK IN AN INTERVIEW AND GUIDES FOR THE INTERVIEWER TAKING NOTES.

<table>
<thead>
<tr class="header">
<th><blockquote>
<p><strong>Example of questions</strong></p>
</blockquote></th>
<th><strong>Notes for the interviewer</strong></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td>Tell me a bit about yourself</td>
<td>This is a very open question that lets the candidate tell you about anything that's important to her/him</td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td>Based on your understanding of the job and our company, how do you feel you'll contribute?</td>
<td>Those who have really prepared will love this opportunity to shine and stand out, demonstrating what they had done????</td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td>Why should we hire you?</td>
<td>Faced with a big stack of resumes telling a similar story, this question can help you determine what sets each candidate apart</td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td>When/if I contact your last supervisor and ask which area of your work needs the most improvement, what will I learn?</td>
<td>It’s the same question as ‘what is your biggest weakness', phrased in an unexpected way</td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td>Tell me about what motivates you, and how do you like to work (solo or on a team, no pressure or lots of pressure, etc.)</td>
<td>If what drives the interviewee matches the position and your culture, it's worth pursuing the conversation</td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td>What frustrates you?</td>
<td>When candidates talk about past frustrations, they reveal details about their personality, diplomacy skills and ability to work on teams. Does the candidate answer by discussing minor irritations - or ways that he successfully resolved serious conflicts over time, budgets, personalities, or priorities?</td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td>What are your strengths? Qualities that people appreciate in you? What are you really good at?</td>
<td>How will they beutilized in your business?</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

| What is really important to you in life? Followed up with: What else?                                                         | People will usually share more if you ask q1 and q2 after a first set of answers                                      |
| What is important to you in a work environment followed up with what else?                                                    | Do they mention pressure or slow pace, friendliness or competition, etc.                                              |
| Where do you see yourself in five years?                                                                                      | Some candidates are ambitious and will be able to answer sincerely. And some will make up a story that sounds good??? |
| For higher positions, ask them to: Follow up with an email telling me what interests you in our company and the open position | And of course, you'll reply to that email, thanking the interviewee and announcing next steps.                        |

### BELOW IS A LIST OF QUESTIONS THAT IS NOT APPROPRIATE TO ASK:

1.  > What is your HIV Status?

2.  > What religion do you practice?

3.  > How old are you?

4.  > What is your sexual orientation?

5.  > What is your marital status?

6.  > What is your family situation?

7.  > What disabilities do you have?

8.  > Where do you live?

9.  > Are you a South African Citizen? Instead, ask if the person is authorized to work in SA

10. > Have you ever been arrested?
