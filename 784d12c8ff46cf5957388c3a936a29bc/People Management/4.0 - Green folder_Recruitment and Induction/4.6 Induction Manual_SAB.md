## 4.6. INDUCTION MANUAL

### Below is a template for an Induction Manual:

Induction Training is absolutely vital for new starters. Good induction training ensures new starters are retained, and then settled in quickly and happily to a productive role.

There are two important elements in induction:

1.  Welcome Pack

2.  Induction Manual

<!-- end list -->

1.  **Welcome pack**

> On arrival of the new employee the Directorate of Human Resources issues a welcome pack all new which contains the following information **(not in order of priority, and only if applicable):**
> 
> Welcome note from Director, Induction details/agenda, Staff details (roles and responsibilities), telephone contact list, maps of sites/office, important passwords, staff cards organisation structure chart, and information on Health and Safety, important forms (e.g. leave).
> 
> You may other important documents you may like to include in your welcome pack.

2.  **Induction Manual**

> The following information listed below is designed to assist the person responsible for induction and ensure it includes essential and appropriate information. Here is a guide as to points you should include in your manual:

1.  > Organisational information (not in order of priority, and only if applicable)

<!-- end list -->

  - > History-organisation

  - > Structure of the Faculty/Directorate

  - > Purpose/role of the Faculty/Directorate

  - > Staff list - names, telephone numbers

  - > Roles and responsibilities of other staff

  - > Annual leave arrangements, who to contact

  - > Sickness arrangements, who and when to inform

  - > Dress/ behaviour code

  - > Tea/coffee, lunch arrangements

  - > Toilet facilities

  - > Faculty/Directorate safety procedures; fire exits and extinguishers, first aid, accidents, safety representatives

  - > Financial Regulations

  - > Arrangement for access to buildings/late working etc.

  - > The internal telephone system/personal calls

  - > Holiday arrangements
    
    2.  > Job information (not in order of priority, and only if applicable)

<!-- end list -->

  - > Duties and responsibilities of the post

  - > Lines of reporting and supervision

  - > Colleagues doing interrelated tasks

  - > Procedural manuals

  - > Sources of funding for training, conferences, research etc.

  - > Personal Development and Review Scheme

  - > Development and training opportunities

  - > Faculty/Directorate meetings

  - > How to obtain equipment/stationery etc.

  - > Hours of work

  - > Work allocation procedures

  - > Secretarial support

  - > Financial Regulations

  - > Examination dates and arrangements

<!-- end list -->

3.  **Feedback**

> Check in with your staff once the induction process is complete.

  - > Was the induction process efficient and effective?

  - > Was the manual useful and easy to understand?

  - > Were the staff members helpful?

  - > Suggestions to improve the transition process

<!-- end list -->

4.  **Some tips\!**

> Induction should take place over a number of weeks, even months. During this time, employees need to assimilate a great deal of information. The Manager responsible for the induction programme can help this programme by:

  - > **Keep things simple-** Provide only the important information/ break information sections as this is more likely to be effective.

  - > **Deciding on Priorities-** What must be said on the new employee’s first day? What can wait for the second week? What will have the most significance in the second month?

  - > **Explaining the purpose of knowing each new piece of Information-**Information is more likely to be digested if the new employee is told the relevance of it to their job or the organisation.

  - > **Using a variety of Techniques-**People have a variety of learning styles. Some people may learn easily from written material. Others may prefer to be given the information orally and also the opportunity to discuss and ask questions.

  - > **Involving other People-**This may include a number of colleagues from the Faculty/Directorate, a representative or someone from another Directorate such as Human Resources, Finance etc.
