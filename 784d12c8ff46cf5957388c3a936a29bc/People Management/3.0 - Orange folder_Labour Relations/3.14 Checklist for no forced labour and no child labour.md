> Companies that respect human rights and implement responsible practices make the most valuable companies with high workplace efficiency. Their reputations are well-regarded and they are seen as attractive employers. It may be difficult to see the difference between work that is performed by children on occasion to help out their families, and other types of work that children do to support themselves or their families for survival, which prevents them from attending compulsory school, a universally recognised right. This checklist will help you guide the entrepreneurs on how to identify and prevent unacceptable approaches to child labour.

## **Definition of Child Labour by the ILO1**

  - > The term “child labour” is often defined as work that deprives children of their childhood, their potential and their dignity, and that is harmful to physical and mental development.

  - > The **worst forms of child labour** involve children being enslaved, separated from their families, exposed to serious hazards and illnesses and/or left to fend for themselves on the streets of large cities – often at a very early age.

> **Child Labour Regulations in South Africa:**

## **The Constitution of South Africa2**

  - > Section 28 of the Constitution specifically protects children from

> exploitative labour practices, from work that is inappropriate for a child’s age, and work that puts the child’s education and physical, mental, spiritual, moral and social development at risk.3

  - > A child ‘means a person under the age of 18 years (section 28(3))’.

## The Basic Conditions of Employment Act4

  - > Children under the age of 15, are protected by Section 43 of the Basic Conditions of Employment Act in that it is a criminal offense to employ a child under the age of 15.

  - > The only exception is if an employer has a permit from the Department of Labour to employ children in the performing arts.

  - > Paragraph 15 of the Guidelines provides that the employer must verify the age of an employee from the identity document or birth certificate.

## South African Schools Acts (84 of 1996)

  - > Section 31(1) provides that a learner must attend school until the last school day of the year in which he/she reaches the age of 15 or grade nine, whichever is first.

## Regulation of the Health & Safety of Children at Work and on Hazardous Work done by Children.

> The Department of Labour has released a comprehensive booklet that clearly lays out the regulations from the BCEA and the OHSA Act around Child Workers. This book can be printed and used as a resource for understanding the regulations. You can download it <span class="underline">here</span>.

  - > It is important to understand the purpose of the regulations, which are to:
    
      - > Protect the health and safety at work of child workers who are lawfully entitled to work.
    
      - > Prohibit categories of work which child workers may not perform.
    
      - > Place conditions on the work that may be performed by child workers.
    
      - > Identify which categories of work constitute worst forms of child labour and to make appropriate enforcement provisions in this regard.

  - > The regulations do not:
    
      - > Permit the employment of any child worker who is under 15 years of age or is subject to compulsory schooling in terms of any law.
    
      - > Reduce any other condition of employment or prohibition applicable to the employment of any person in the Act or any other law, insofar as it applies to the employment of a child.

  - > An employer who employs a child worker must ensure that their employment does not interfere with:
    
      - > Their access to adequate nutrition.
    
      - > Their access to adequate primary health care services.
    
      - > The education of a child worker who is enrolled at a school or at any other educational institution.

## Work away from Parents or Guardians:

  - > A child worker may only perform work which will result in its being away overnight from its parents or legal guardian:
    
      - > If the child’s parent or legal guardian consents in writing.
    
      - > If the child is enrolled in school, that the schooling of a school-going child is not adversely affected.

  - > In addition, the employer must provide:
    
      - > Full details of the accommodation arrangements to the parent or legal guardian of the child worker for their approval

## Prohibition of Piecework or Task Work

  - > No person may require or permit a child worker to perform piecework or task work.

  - > Piecework is work in terms of which remuneration is based mainly on the quantity of work done.

  - > Task work is work in terms of which the remuneration of a worker is based mainly on the completion of set tasks.

## Night Work

  - > A child worker may not work before 06:00 or after 18:00 on any day, except where allowed

  - > A child worker, other than one who is expected to be at school the following day, may work between 18:00 and 23:00 if:
    
      - > The work is in a restaurant, cinema, theatre or shop where there is adequate adult supervision
    
      - > The work is baby-sitting or child minding.
    
      - > Unless the parent or legal guardian agrees otherwise in writing, any person who requires or permits a child worker to work after 18:00 must, at the end of the shift, provide him/her with safe transport home, at no cost to the child, parent or care-giver

## Child Labour General Pointers

  - > Define the (or your) business use of children for labour. Then compare those limits to the international laws and national laws governing the use of children for work. Are there differences between your views and the law?

  - > Before you hire a young person, consider asking for proof of the applicant’s

> age and provide a contract that requires guardian signature/s.

  - > Young workers under the age of 18 should have assigned points of contact.

## General Guidance

  - > All workers must be provided with written terms and conditions of employment and benefits for their review and signature before they begin work; and for migrant workers, this should be before they leave their home country.

  - > Workers must be paid their wages in full and on time as mutually agreed upon.

  - > Workers must not be charged recruitment, processing or placement fees to obtain employment.

  - > If required, employers should work with reputable and accredited recruitment and employment agencies, where there are procedures in place to check their practices and policies.

  - > Workers must be allowed to keep their passports and other personal documents.

  - > Workers must be free to leave the worksite and their accommodation when they are not working.

## Contracting

  - > Written contracts are drafted and provided to the employee to check and give feedback on - even for temporary work\!

  - > Visual contracts or recordings should be used for those employees who are illiterate.

  - > All contracts must have:
    
      - > Personal details (names)
    
      - > Date of signature
    
      - > Job description
    
      - > Duration of the contract
    
      - > If applicable: price per piece, time spent per piece and number of pieces
    
      - > If applicable: working hours and overtime conditions
    
      - > Payment and payment conditions
    
      - > Benefits
    
      - > Conditions to termination with/without notice.

  - > Housing, food, childcare, training, and/or transportation cannot be deducted from salaries.

  - > Workers must be free to leave their employment, without penalty, on giving reasonable notice (in accordance with national law and the contract).

  - > Workers must not be forced to work overtime above national limits.

  - > Employers must make sure that there is a confidential and safe grievance mechanism that can be used and trusted by workers.

  - > Workers must retain their agency and choice about where they live or with whom they live.

  - > If you provide room and board, the living conditions must be secure, safe and clean.
