## 3.10. MANAGING PERFORMANCE

> **GENERAL GUIDELINES**
> 
> The management of the standards of work performance of our employees is the most important aspect of our job as a manager. It sets the tone for the overall performance of our business. There are a number of reasons as to why employee work performance may deteriorate or not match our requirements or standards. These usually are:

  - > the employee does not have the necessary skills or knowledge. This is remedied through training or through on-job coaching.

  - > the employee’s attitude towards his / her job is unacceptable and or their levels of motivation are lacking

> Generally speaking, issues of poor performance would first be addressed with an employee in an informal manner through discussions and informal meetings. If the poor performance continues, or be of a more serious nature, then a more structured, recorded counseling session should be held with the employee. This counseling session will be structured as a GROW session (see <span class="underline">Performance Counselling Form – GROW</span> proforma).
> 
> Failure to improve performance after counseling, or in cases of serious poor performance, will lead to a Performance Enquiry (see <span class="underline">Performance Enquiry Form</span> proforma).
> 
> **STEPS TO FOLLOWED DURING A PERFORMANCE COUNSELLING SESSION**

1.  > Privately explain the purpose of the counselling (GOAL)

> The purpose of this performance counselling is to correct and improve performance which is below standard by giving feedback, evaluation, counseling and guidance.

2.  > Give feedback on satisfactory and unsatisfactory performance (REALITY)

<!-- end list -->

  - > Be positive and give praise where due

  - > Describe shortcomings and details the standards expected

  - > Give the employee an opportunity to respond

<!-- end list -->

3.  > Discuss ways in which shortcomings can be rectified (OPTIONS)

<!-- end list -->

  - > Discuss with the employee all viable options to overcome shortcomings, e.g. training, coaching, further assistance etc.

<!-- end list -->

4.  > Agree on an action plan to rectify shortcomings (WRAP UP)

<!-- end list -->

  - > Jointly agree on an action plan to address shortcomings

  - > Issue employee with <span class="underline">Verbal/Written/Final Written Warning</span> pro-forma

  - > Determine date of next probationary counselling / performance enquiry

  - > Express confidence in the employee (if appropriate)

> **STEPS TO FOLLOWED DURING A PERFORMANCE ENQUIRY**

1.  > Preparation for the Performance Enquiry
    
    1.  > Prior to any Performance Enquiry, the employee’s immediate manager should investigate employee’s work performance and prepare the background that will be put to the employee during the Enquiry. The manager should use the <span class="underline">Initiator’s Preparation Bundle</span> pro-forma.
    
    2.  > The investigation should identify where employee’s performance does not meet required performance standards.
    
    3.  > The investigation should **quantify the difference** between expected and actual performance eg that the employee should be able to clean six rooms per day but is only at present cleaning four rooms.
    
    4.  > The employee should then be **advised in writing** of the intention to hold a Performance Enquiry. The <span class="underline">Notification</span> pro-forma letter contained in your manual should be used for this purpose.

<!-- end list -->

1.  > Conduct the Performance EnquiryThe manager conducting the Performance Enquiry, the Chairperson, may use the <span class="underline">Performance Enquiry Form</span> to guide him / her through the Performance Enquiry.
    
    1.  > The Chairperson should open the Enquiry by detailing the allegations of poor or unacceptable work performance as contained in the Notification Letter.
    
    2.  > The Chairperson should then confirm the employee’s rights at the Performance Enquiry

<!-- end list -->

  - > To be represented by a fellow employee / shop steward of the employee’s choice

  - > State his / her case in response
    
    1.  > The immediate manager should confirm that the employee has received the Notification Letter, understands the allegations of poor performance and has had sufficient time to prepare a response.
    
    2.  > The immediate manager should **detail the performance standards** required from the employee.
    
    3.  > The immediate manager should explain why these performance standards are **reasonable and necessary.**
    
    4.  > The immediate manager should detail how employee **was made aware** / could reasonably be expected to be aware of performance standards.
    
    5.  > The immediate manager should detail how employee’s **performance differs** from that required.
    
    6.  > The immediate manager should detail **what induction / instruction** procedures have been followed.
    
    7.  > The immediate manager should detail training / coaching provided / evaluations /counseling sessions held.
    
    8.  > The immediate manager should detail:

<!-- end list -->

  - > how **assessments of employee’s progress** were made & how these were fed back to employee

  - > any **further reasonable action** which could be taken to build competency
    
    1.  > The Employee or employee representative should be requested to detail:

<!-- end list -->

  - > **any response** to the facts & opinions put forward by the Chairperson

  - > any **further issues** or action plans he/she believes are appropriate
    
    1.  > Chairperson should **consider the circumstances** as well as the case put forward by the employee / representative and answer the following questions in making the decision as to whether the employee’s performance is indeed poor or unacceptable.

<!-- end list -->

  - > Did the employee’s contract of employment require him / her to **meet the required performance standards**?

<!-- end list -->

  - > Were these standards **reasonable**?

  - > Was it **probable** that the employee’s performance did not meet these required standards?

  - > Was the employee made **aware** of the **required performance standards**?

  - > Was the employee given **assistance – coaching / training / evaluation / guidance**?

  - > Where appropriate was the employee **given an opportunity to improve**?

  - > Were **regular assessments** of performance made & fed back to employee?

  - > Have all **reasonable opportunities** been afforded to the employee?

  - > Has the employee **failed** despite this to meet the required standards?

  - > Do any **alternative positions** exist in which the employee could perform competently?

  - > Is a further action plan to assist the employee to meet the required standard appropriate?

  - > Is termination of employment appropriate in the circumstances?

<!-- end list -->

2.  > Inform the employee of the outcome of the Performance Enquiry

> The Chairperson should make his / her decision known to employee in writing – Use <span class="underline">Performance Enquiry – Summary of Outcome</span> (A or B, as contained in the Performance Enquiry proforma in your manual).
> 
> *If the employee is to be warned, then;*

  - > an action plan to improve the employee’s performance should be agreed upon

  - > a further Enquiry Date should be set

  - > assistance should be offered

> If the employee’s conditions of employment are to be amended;

  - > the changes should be discussed and agreed in writing with the employee.

> If the employee is to be dismissed;

  - > confirm that the employee’s contract of employment will be terminated with effect from a certain date.

  - > **Advise employee** of his/her right to refer the matter to CCMA or relevant

  - > Bargaining Council for conciliation / arbitration.
