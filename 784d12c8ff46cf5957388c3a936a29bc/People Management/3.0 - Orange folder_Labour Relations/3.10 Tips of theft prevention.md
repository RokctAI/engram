**Preventing Theft in your Organisation**

One of the best ways to prevent theft is to make sure that your employees feel valued and are rewarded correctly. Happy employees are less likely to steal.

However, within the South African work environment, theft seems to be something that employees think is okay. This can often be attributed to unclear definitions within the workplace. Within this context, the first area you need to look at is your policies and procedures. If these are not yet in place, look at the sample provided below and adapt it for your business as a starting point.

For those businesses with existing policies and procedures, have you clearly defined what theft is in your Code of Conduct? If not, please update. If yes, please enforce it.

Sometimes theft is about temptation and the opportunity. Here are some measures that you could consider implementing in your workplace:

  - > In areas where people work with cash, have you secured your processes to make it difficult for someone to steal?

  - > Is your stock control well managed to minimise opportunities for theft?

  - > Although we want to encourage a climate of trust, you need to behave like there is no trust when it comes to monitoring staff and customer movement to ensure that nothing can be stolen. Practically, you may need to have a locker room with lockers installed where staff can leave their handbags, etc in a safe and secure place. This area should be secure and ideally accessed from a different entrance to your shop floor. This means that when staff leave the premises, they should carry nothing.

  - > Can you install cameras in your store/business to monitor movements and curb theft?

  - > Change your staff shifts or have random shifts where possible, this keeps your staff movements random in order to minimise any opportunities for them to get too familiar with customers.

  - > Have a monthly walk around your premises and “think like a thief” to minimise the opportunities for theft. Be open minded to change your processes until you find what is watertight.

  - > In certain circumstances make one person responsible for certain items, to avoid confusion and opportunity for blame e.g., A toolbox should belong to one person, so if tools go missing, you can question the one person, not the entire workshop as that is a recipe for no one to take responsibility.

  - > Secure and encrypt all electronic equipment especially mobile devices to ensure that you if stolen, you won’t be liable for any data breach.

  - > If you have meetings, have stock shrinkage or stock theft as an item on the agenda, and discuss this. Where possible link it to the salary conversation e.g., Stock losses could mean no bonus, or no stock losses means a bonus for all. Reward the right behaviour to encourage the honest culture and build a happy team\!

> **Here are some sample clauses which you could add to your Code of Conduct:**

1.  > Theft of any property from YOUR COMPANY NAME, its staff or clients will not be tolerated. Individuals discovered stealing will be passed to the SAPS for prosecution. This is in accordance with Section 34 (2) of POCA (Prevention of Organised Crime). Theft is defined as the taking of items, money, or something of value belonging to someone else, with the intention of depriving them of it.

2.  > Items of value which if taken can constitute theft include, but are not limited to:
    
    1.  > Airtime loaded onto YOUR COMPANY NAME or client phones.
    
    2.  > Data loaded onto YOUR COMPANY NAME or client phones.
    
    3.  > Using YOUR COMPANY NAME or client airtime or data for personal reasons without prior permission.

3.  > Wastage of YOUR COMPANY NAME or client supplied materials includes but is not limited to:
    
    1.  > Use of YOUR COMPANY NAME or client supplied telephones for personal calls.
    
    2.  > Use of YOUR COMPANY NAME or client supplied ‘smart devices’, laptops, tablets or computers for personal use or downloading data.
    
    3.  > Using YOUR COMPANY NAME or client supplied ‘paperwork’ or pre-printed forms as ‘scribble paper’.
    
    4.  > Using any YOUR COMPANY NAME or client supplied materials repeatedly for purposes that they were not designed for, eg using toilet paper for blowing a person’s nose (this is an employee’s responsibility, not an employer’s responsibility).
    
    5.  > Using equipment or materials for personal use, without permission, causing them to be ‘worn out’ and not fit for their supplied function. Eg. using the office broom at a private residence.
