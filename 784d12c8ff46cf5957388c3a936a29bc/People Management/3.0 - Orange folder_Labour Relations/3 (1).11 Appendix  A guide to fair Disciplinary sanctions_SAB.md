Do you have an employee who is just not doing anything? An employee who is taking it easy while still expecting the rewards of working hard? Well, if you have such an employee, you may want to take drastic action. But hold on, you can't just one day decide 'that's it, you're out\!' There is performance management and disciplinary procedure to be followed in a situation like this.

This guideline offer an overview of the sequence of events and procedures that should be in place prior to initiating dismissal.

### PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT AND COUNSELLING

> **Here are 8 steps that you should implement as part of the overall performance management process. These steps should be documented, which will ensure that the background case for dismissal is clear, should you decide to terminate a contract, based on poor performance.**

1.  > Set performance standards

<!-- end list -->

  - > Give your employee a detailed job description that sets out clearly what she's required to do and what level of performance is required

  - > Lay out Key Performance Areas (KPAs) which describe the main areas of the job the employee is required to focus on.

  - > Alongside setting time frames, you may want to set clear-cut targets for the employee. This is especially appropriate in sales, where you can measure sales in specific rand terms over a particular period of time.

<!-- end list -->

2.  > Communicate those performance standards to your employee.

3.  > Evaluate your employee's performance standards.

4.  > Ensure you've given her sufficient instructions with regard to what you want and according to how you want it.

5.  > Provide any training the employee requires to improve. Unless, of course, you hire her on the basis of a qualification, with which it's reasonably expected that she'll know what to do.

6.  > Provide guidance along the way in order to help any areas in which the employee seeks to improve.

7.  > Counsel her on her performance and, in a joint problem-solving way, find ways to achieve your *reasonable* standards.

8.  > Give your employee a reasonable period of time to meet the reasonable standards.

> Only embark on the process for dismissal if you have used up all ways to get your employee to meet your required, and of course reasonable, standards. You must enable a process give your employee the opportunity to make representations before you dismiss her. This process should be documented.
> 
> *Remember: you need to follow the correct procedure when dismissing an employee for poor performance. Failure to do so can lead to unfair dismissal and a trip to the CCMA.*

### DISCIPLINARY HEARINGS

> Having to hold a disciplinary hearing isn't pleasant. No employer enjoys going head to head with an employee. But this is a necessary step when an employee commits misconduct and you must do everything by the book to avoid landing at the CCMA and having the hearing ruled as unfair.
> 
> Any area of misconduct according to your company list of sanctions (refer to section IV below) should be handled through a disciplinary hearing. This enables a formal enquiry and a documentation of process, which is essential for managing potential CCMA cases. This also gives written record for a case file including a written warning. After three written warnings, you are within your rights to dismiss (provided you follow the correct process).
> 
> **Scenario:**
> 
> ***An employee has committed a serious / dismissible offence, what do I do? HELP, I do not want to go to the CCMA\!* So where do you start?**
> 
> You must prepare\!
> 
> Here are the three things you must do to prepare for a disciplinary hearing so you can ensure it is legally compliant.

1.  > **Consider suspending your employee while you investigate the matter **

> Your employee can continue with his job while you're still investigating. But in some cases there could be a potential danger in keeping your employee at work.
> 
> For example, if s/he's suspected of assault, stealing petty cash or committing an act that threatens people, property, operations, industrial relations or information. If you believe s/he may interfere with witnesses or tamper with evidence, suspend her/him temporarily.

2.  > **Formulate charges**

> After gathering all the facts, formulate the charges you want to bring against your employee. The charge must contain details of who, what, when, where, and why. This must be clear so there's no argument from your employee that s/he didn't have sufficient details to prepare a defence.

3.  > **Prepare your strategy:**

> Once you're done with the investigation and formulating charges, organise the evidence. Prepare in such a way as to provide a full, clear and watertight case against the accused. This aspect of the preparation involves:

1.  > Sorting and ordering of evidence;

2.  > Selection of witnesses' documents and other evidence to be used; and

3.  > Deciding how you'll sequence and interweave the evidence for the best effect.

*Note: Preparation is key when it comes to a **disciplinary hearing.** So make sure you follow these three steps to prepare so your hearing will be legally compliant.*

### DURING THE HEARING

  - > Give the accused an opportunity to plead to the charges.

  - > Make sure the complainant puts his case first, leading evidence and calling witnesses to testify.

  - > Give the accused an opportunity to cross question witnesses.

  - > Make sure the accused leads evidence in her/his defence.

  - > Ensure the accused calls her/his witnesses to testify.

  - > Give the complainant an opportunity to cross question accused’s witnesses.

  - > The Chairperson must adjourn the hearing for at least 3 days to have minutes typed up or transcribed.

  - > The accused must be handed a copy of the minutes.

  - > The Chairperson must consider whether a fair procedure has been followed. S/he must decide on guilt or innocence based on the evidence presented by both sides and on the balance of probability – which story is more likely to be true? That of the complainant or that of the accused?

  - > The Chairperson must reconvene the hearing. S/he must advise the accused of the guilty verdict. If not guilty, this must be confirmed in writing to the accused and the matter must be closed.

  - > If guilty, the Chairperson must ask the accused if s/he has anything to add in mitigation of sentence.

  - > The Chairperson must adjourn meeting again to consider any mitigating facts now added.

  - > The Chairperson must consider and decide on a fair sanction. S/he must then reconvene the hearing to deliver the sanction.

  - > The Chairperson must advise the accused of her/his rights to appeal and to refer the matter to the CCMA.

> Make sure all communication to the accused, such as the verdict, the sanction, advice of his rights is in writing. This will help you if you have to go to the CCMA.Your employee’s disciplinary hearing will only be procedurally fair if you adhere to the points outlined in this checklist.
