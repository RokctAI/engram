## 16. Small Business Compliance Basic Information Sheet

### PAYE

*   Every employer (person, partnership or company who employs at least one person) that has an employee that earns above R7 980.00 per month, is required to apply to SARS, for registration as an employer within 21 business days of becoming an employer in order to pay Employee's Tax.
*   One-person businesses with no employees do not have to register for PAYE. That individual would be classified as a provisional taxpayer.
*   Employee's Tax is the tax required to be deducted or withheld by the employer from the remuneration paid/payable to an employee as it is earned by the employee, this is known as Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE).
*   The PAYE amount withheld from the employees must be declared Monthly Employer Declaration Form (EMP201) and paid over to SARS on or before the 7th of the following month.
*   Employers must provide employees with an IRP5 form which discloses the tax deducted from their salaries.
*   An employer who is required to register with SARS for PAYE is also required to register for Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) contributions (more detail is provided below).
*   The following documents will be needed for registration:
    *   Application Forms (Available on the SARS e-filing website or at any SARS branch)
        *   EMP 101e – Payroll Taxes – Application for Registration or
        *   EMP 102e – Payroll Taxes – Application for Branch Registration
    *   Employers who are registered for Employee's Tax must submit an Employer Reconciliation Declaration (EMP501) biannually as follows:
        *   **Interim period** – which is for the six-month period 1 March to 31 August, and this is due 31st October each year.
        *   **Annual period** – which if for the full year 1 March to 28/29 February and this is due 30th April each year.

### Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF)

*   You are required by law to register with the Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) and contribute towards the fund if you have employed people who work for you for more than 24 hours per month.
*   One-person business with no employees does not have to register for UIF.
*   It is the employer's responsibility to register each of their employees for UIF, unless an employee is one of the following:
    *   An employee working less than 24 hours per month for a single employer.
    *   Foreigners working on contract; or
    *   Workers who only earn commission
*   Employers must pay UIF contributions of 2% of the value of each worker’s monthly remuneration: 1% is deducted from the employee’s salary and 1% is contributed by the employer.
*   The payment must be made to the UIF or SARS before the 7th of every month (for the previous month’s contributions).
*   Employees are entitled to claim benefits from the Fund at the Department of Labour in the event that they become unemployed for reasons beyond their control. This includes the payment of illness, maternity, adoption and dependant’s benefits related to the unemployment of those employees (if benefits are not being received from the Compensation Fund in this regard).
*   When an employee gets fired or goes on maternity leave the employee can then claim UIF benefits at the department of labour.
