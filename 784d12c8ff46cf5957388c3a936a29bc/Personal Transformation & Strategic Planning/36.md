SMART goals were developed by George Doran, Arthur Miller and James Cunningham in their **1981 article “There’s a S.M.A.R.T. way to write management goals and objectives”**

---

### Key Performance Indicators (KPI’s)

Each and every one of us has experience in personal goal setting – we have all made plans to lose weight, give up smoking, eat healthier, go to gym....

But the telling factor is how many of us achieve these goals or targets? It’s like setting New Years’ Resolutions – which are more often than not forgotten before 31st January!

There are many reasons why so many of us fail to reach so many of our goals and the way to increase your success rate is to create SMART goals and targets, ones that are:

*   **SPECIFIC:** And clearly defined
*   **MEASURABLE:** Without an indication of whether we are on track or not, how will we know if we will ever achieve our goals?
*   **ACHIEVABLE:** Set realistic Goals and Dreams and not pipedreams! Whilst goals should stretch us a bit, setting goals that are too high will demoralize you and your team
*   **RELEVANT:** Poorly aligned Goals will not help you succeed. Make sure the goals you set are relevant to your purpose
*   **TIMEBOUND:** Your goals should have a specific end-date against which to measure performance

The way to ensure we stay on track and implement the Strategic Action Plan we set for ourselves and our business, is to set ourselves a number of smaller targets that need to be achieved if our Action Plan is to be realised.

**For example,** if one of your Strategic Objectives is to ‘Reduce overheads by 10% in the next twelve months’, a good way to ensure you attain this is by having a KPI that says ‘Tenant in place to share rental costs by 1st May.’ If you have this in place, you would have taken a big step towards reducing overheads.
