Introduction - Vision Setting for Success
A successful journey starts with an understanding of where you are going, and why you are going there. Even if
you do not have a set itinerary or are keen to keep things flexible, having a clear idea of where you are headed
is crucial to your chances of getting there. Without direction, you can get lost.
Growing a business is the same. Unless you have an idea of what you are trying to achieve through your business
and all the work you put in, your chances of creating that desired future are very slim. This is why having a clear
Vision - and a plan in place to achieve it - is so important to your long-term success.
A vision will help you steer in a focused direction and be the captain of your own boat, and a plan will help you
stay on track and ultimately reach your destination!
‘If you don’t know where you’re going, any road will get you there.’ – Lewis Carroll
1. Principle #1 – Know yourself, know your business
This personal transformation and strategic planning workshop is carefully constructed to take you through two
essential parts of your growth journey.
The first part is your own personal transformation & growth. We will help you unlock a deeper understanding
of who you are and what you are on earth to achieve, by guiding you through a process of personal reflection and
increased self-awareness. This is an important step towards building self-mastery, which is an essential business
leadership skill.
As you work through this personal transformation process, we will help you to strengthen the link between your
‘self’ and your business. This will further build inspiration and strengthen your commitment to lasting success.
Lastly, we will help you to build a powerful, actionable strategic growth plan for your business that has all
the elements you need to realize your dreams!
Many small businesses start from a simple idea and get going without too much thought about the future. Entrepreneurs
are known for their ability to just jump in and get started, and we tend to worry about the details later.
‘If you fail to plan, you are planning to fail!’ Benjamin Franklin
Even those organisations that do begin with a solid plan can find themselves without direction some years later.
Perhaps the original leader or champion has left, the environment has changed, or things have just become a bit
fuzzy over time.

model that we use to identify the priorities and focus areas to create forward momentum. The Plan on a Page

This Plan on a page will form the basis of our work together over the coming months.

Wheel Scorecard and will help you to create focus and direction in your organisation. A template is provided
that you will complete during this workshop and refine further as the programme progresses.

5. Principle #5 - Mastering the Magic of Money!
Money is on everyone’s mind but is not everyone’s favourite topic. While some people hold a positive attitude
towards money, many others have limiting, often unconscious beliefs around money that hold them back. These
beliefs are formed and shaped during our formative years as we observe how our parents and caregivers work
with and feel about money. Sometimes our feelings towards money are so strong that we start to believe that money
is the cause of, or solution to all of our problems. That’s the power of money.
Rationale
As entrepreneurs your dream is to create a successful, profitable businesses. Yet so often this dream becomes stuck
and the success we seek, remains just out of reach. The Money Magic roadmap helps you to recognize how your
personal relationship to money – how you feel about it and how it makes you feel influences this success. Using
this powerful realisation you can step more confidently into the dos, don’ts and how to’s of financial management.
Getting to grips with the importance of record-keeping and managing the money inside the business. In step three
we help you to put this all together to access the finance you need to grow the business.
Our goal is that by the time you complete the Tholoana Enterprise Programme you have the confidence, skills and
documents you need to access level 1 – scored credit (e.g. to buy a car on credit). To do this you will need to make
a commitment to change your attitude towards money and become comfortable with your finances. By addressing
these matters and becoming fully confident to master your money you will ensure your success, not just for today,
but for a lifetime.
The 3-step Money Magic Journey
To Master the magic of money we will follow a simple 3 step process. Money Magic, Financial Management and
Investment readiness. Let’s break these down below:

The Money Magic Journey begins with understanding your own intimate relationship to money and how this affects
the way you manage your personal finances.
The Financial Roadmap helps you to set a clear framework for your Money Magic transformation. Beginning with
easy look at your goals for the next 2 years. Do you plan to purchase a new fridge, a car, or a property? Whatever
it is, we will work together to develop a Financial Roadmap to get you from where you are now to where you need
to be to achieve these goals.
Next, we invite you to attend two very empowering webinars that address the emotions of money and You and
your Money. These two webinars will help you face your financial fears by clarifying your money beliefs and
empower you with game-changing strategies to grow your personal wealth. Group personal finance coaching is
also available for additional practical guidance.
This new knowledge of your own personal relationship with money will empower you in conversations with you
Mentor who will provide guidance to further develop your business financial roadmap and schedule a site visit
with a qualified accountant to review your financial systems and process and empower you with some key action

7.1. People
Human Resources: “Without people there is no business” is a frequently made statement, and this is true!
Every single business needs people – even highly automated ones that run on the internet! It is people that make
the decisions in the business, and usually people that design the products, provide the services and interact with

Leadership: People also refers to you and your skills, as the leader or senior person in your organization. Your

7.2. Systems and Processes
Operational & QMS: Not everyone thinks of business success in terms of its ‘processes’ and quality management
systems. But a business that wants to grow needs strong systems and methods – these are the building blocks to
growth. So, whilst people in a business may come and go, it is the regular and repeated, efficient systems and
processes that keep the business working.
A business wanting to expand must develop systems that can be replicated. This includes things such as job
descriptions, production methods, sales systems, quality standards, even things like telephone etiquette and dress
code. Systems help run the business and maintain quality standards.
Technology & Communications: Technology and IT is an increasingly important component of many businesses
– often technology can reduce the cost of a product or service, making it more competitive and attractive to
customers. IT, communications and databases are all parts of the process component.
7.3. Finance & Governance
Financial Sustainability & Growth: Financial performance hardly needs any explanation I would think.
Financial performance is all about improving profit margins, cutting costs, increasing sales, managing cash flow.
Any organisation, including non-profits must be masters of their financial performance, for the simple reality is that
a business lives or dies on its financial results. Learning how to build a financially efficient and profitable business
is critical to success.
Legislation & Compliance: A big part of effective financial management is sound financial administration,
including legislative compliance and governance processes. Effective governance, and legislative compliance forms
an important part of audit reports, and thorough admin in general is essential for any business, from an operational
and governance perspective.
7.4. Customers
Without customers you have no business, so customers are without doubt a critical component of success. This is
true even for a non-profit, NGO or even a Church. Customers bring money into the organisation – they can be
customers for a product or service, or donors and supporters. Every business needs to serve their customers well.
Every business needs their customer to keep coming back.
Products & Services: Are your products or services aligned to the market you have identified as having the most
potential for your business? Are you continuing to innovate, improve and advance your offering in order to meet the
needs of your customers?
Sales & Marketing: it’s the goal of a business to continually find and keep customers. How effective is your sales
and marketing? Do you have the tools you need to succeed in this area? Are you bringing in the sales you need to

Leaders are often described as visionary people, inspiring confidence and influencing others. But there are many

Being effective as a leader means understanding your style and staying true to it, especially in a small business
where a leader can make or break success.
Finding your own voice comes first. The journey to be a truly great leader starts with understanding yourself….
Leadership
Leadership is an essential skill for any successful entrepreneur - whether leading others or just yourself. In fact, no
leader can effectively lead others if they are unable lead themselves.
Leadership is not a role that can be assigned to somebody – it is a personal choice that is driven by an inner desire
and long-term commitments
A leader does not choose his or her followers – it is the followers who choose their leaders!
Epic leaders demonstrate four common attributes:

The Fetola EPIC Leaders model
1. E: They lead by example by living the values and behaviours that they would expect from their followers

2. P: They lead with a purpose – they understand and are passionately aligned to the organisation’s quest

3. I: They are inspirational – people believe in them, what they do and what they represent, and want to

4. C: They are consistent - leadership is not a part time activity, as leaders are under constant observation

Core Values & Behaviours
As a business leader the energy that you send into the world is critical. As you grow, the ability to create a team
around you that has the same energy becomes an integral part of your organisation.
This energy derives from the centre of what you believe is important, and it can be expressed in terms of your core
values (the beliefs on which the organisation is built). These align closely to your leadership approach and style.
Core values help you share what is important to the organization, both to your team and to your customers.
We all have a set of personal values or beliefs that govern how we behave, and how we believe others should
behave. In reality we don’t actually see these values in a person, we only see their behaviour. We all know
examples of people who say one thing and do another – and how off-putting this can be...
Remember, it is not what you SAY that is important, it is what you DO that counts.
Our core values are important, but our behaviour is critical!

For this reason, identifying your core values needs to be a two- step process. Ideally this is done as a team, so that
the organisation is aligned in their thinking:
• Step One – Choose a value or belief that you feel is important in your business. Examples include ethics,
trust, transparency, fairness, innovation, quality, service, reliability etc.
• Step Two – Write down how you will (or do) turn this value or belief into action.
This second step is very important, as action is more important than words. Remember that the behaviour needs to
extend throughout the organisation, not just in the leadership.
Example:
Value = “Quality”
Problem = How to make Quality happen all through the business?
Action = Set quality standards in all areas of the business, implement quality management systems,
measure customer complaints, provide quality training to each staff member, etc.
Follow this process in your own time to determine your own set of company Values, and how you will turn them
into reality through action.
Ethics in Leadership
Former Chief Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court Potter Stewart stated: “Ethics is knowing the difference between

All companies stand to profit from a reputation for acting with honesty and integrity. Ethical behaviour in business
can ensure long-term sustainability and respectability.
As a business leader, your behaviour demonstrates the ethical beliefs and practices of your company. It can
directly influence the values and ethics of your staff and employees, affecting everything from company culture to
sales and investments.
So essentially “walk the talk”.
Some points to consider:
• What do ethics mean to you?
• Do ethics apply in your personal and business life? Both?
• Are your stakeholders affected by your decisions/actions?

The Importance of Culture
Leadership, core values, behaviours and ethics all feed into your company culture.
In essence, company culture is the personality of a company. It defines the environment in which employees work.
Company culture includes a variety of elements, including work environment, company mission, value, ethics,
expectations, and goals.
As the world becomes more connected through technology, businesses need to work so much harder to gain a
sustainable competitive advantage. In the past, creating a great product, coupled with excellent service was
enough to keep you at the top of your game.
However, the marketplace has changed, and conditions have become more volatile. Competitors are constantly
developing new innovations and can easily imitate your products or services. Technology is evolving at a rapid

SMART goals were developed by George Doran, Arthur Miller and James Cunningham in their 1981 article
“There’s a S.M.A.R.T. way to write management goals and objectives”
Key Performance Indicators (KPI’s)
Each and every one of us has experience in personal goal setting – we have all made plans to lose weight, give
up smoking, eat healthier, go to gym....
But the telling factor is how many of us achieve these goals or targets? It’s like setting New Years’ Resolutions –
which are more often than not forgotten before 31st January!
There are many reasons why so many of us fail to reach so many of our goals and the way to increase your success
rate is to create SMART goals and targets, ones that are:
SPECIFIC And clearly defined
MEASURABLE Without an indication of whether we are on track or not, how will we know if we will ever achieve
our goals?
ACHIEVABLE Set realistic Goals and Dreams and not pipedreams! Whilst goals should stretch us a bit, setting goals that
are too high will demoralize you and your team
RELEVANT Poorly aligned Goals will not help you succeed. Make sure the goals you set are relevant to your purpose
TIMEBOUND Your goals should have a specific end-date against which to measure performance
The way to ensure we stay on track and implement the Strategic Action Plan we set for ourselves and our business,
is to set ourselves a number of smaller targets that need to be achieved if our Action Plan is to be realised.
For example, if one of your Strategic Objectives is to ‘Reduce overheads by 10% in the next twelve months’, a
good way ensure you attain this is by having a KPI that says ‘Tenant in place to share rental costs by 1st May.’ If
you have this in place, you would have taken a big step towards reducing overheads.

15. Bringing Sustainable Practices into Your Business
There’s an assumption that growing the economy will lead to improving people’s lives.
On a national scale, the wellbeing of citizens is considered to be tied to how much a country makes in a year
(i.e. Gross Domestic Products - GDP = growth). This can be deceptive, since increases in GDP often come from a
minority of high-income earners, and the majority who earn very little are hardly ever counted.
At Fetola, we know that not having access to capital is only one part of a more complex network of issues
that people in South Africa face, including environmental changes, depleting resources and social conflict. As
entrepreneurs, these issues can have very real impacts on business.
Guidelines for Going Green
We present simple guidelines for more sensitive businesses practices. Some of these will make sense from a cost-
saving perspective (water saving and energy saving) and many of these are simply good practices.
Some of these might work for you from a cost-saving perspective or could even present an opportunity to market
your business differently.
Why go green? What’s in it for me?
Will going green help you to sleep better at night? Perhaps – but not if it’s costing you more than you can afford
to spend. Read on for some comforting insights into the benefits for you and your business:
• Direct cost-savings: Waste reduction and water conservation, as well as reduced energy and resources, all
result in financial savings.
• Employee productivity: There is a connection between meaningful work and productivity.
• Staff health: environmentally friendly products (detergents, chemicals, paints, etc.) are better for staff health,
compared with hazardous chemicals and toxic substances. They also make the office environment more
pleasant (fewer odours, less irritation for eyes, nose, and throat), which has a positive impact on productivity.
• Social benefits: there is a strong link between environmental degradation and social problems. By ensuring
that your office does not harm the environment, or working to actively green or enhance the environment, you
contribute to a healthier society.
• You could well end up attracting new customers – there are people who care enough about these issues to
make purchasing decisions around the issue of environmental responsibility (e.g., choosing an item with less
packaging).
Your business: Practical Eco-tips
Paper
Paper eventually biodegrades in landfill sites (it is often seen as a better packaging option for this reason). But the
production of paper is resource-intensive, requiring water, forest resources and energy – not to mention the air and
waste emissions associated with paper production.
• Recycle your paper: Set used paper aside, shred it and arrange for collection or make a monthly trip to a
recycling depot.
© 2024 Fetola (Pty) Ltd • Phone: 0861111690 • sabfoundationtholoana@fetola.co.za 54

• Paper flyers - make sure you indicate that these are recyclable: If you must print flyers for your
business, make sure you use gloss-free paper, and clearly print the phrase: “Do not litter” and “This flyer is
recyclable” on the reverse. Though it may be obvious Gross Domestic Products (GDP) growth to some, may
come as significant cost to others, many people need that reminder.
• Print in an environmentally-friendly way: Make sure you’re printing in a way that has a minimal effect
on the environment – reverse-sided, black and white. For internal office work use scrap paper.
• Use shredded scrap paper for shipping or packing. When shipping breakable marketing materials
(i.e., promotional mugs) use your scrap paper as packing materials -- just be sure to run all scrap paper through
a shredder first.
Transport
• Buy locally wherever possible: This will minimize the transport costs – both the freight cost and the cost
to the environment.
• Sell online: While that might sound obvious, many small businesses don’t sell their products online. Selling
online is a must both for your business and the environment. It instantly expands your customer base from just
your local neighbourhood to the entire world! The power to ship your product greenly is within your control
(we’ll get to that).
• Use a smaller and more fuel-efficient car.
Go durable - Less wastage
• Buy promotional products that are durable: Instead of buying branded, bottled water in disposable bottles - a
growing trend - order branded, reusable coffee mugs or branded, reusable water bottles. Not only will you
avoid adding to millions of tons of plastic landfill waste, but your marketing message will be noticed for far
longer because your coffee mugs and/or water bottles will be reused.
Energy savings
The hikes in energy costs between 1 April 2013 and 31 March 2018 approved by NERSA represent an increase
of at least 47% in the electricity costs for businesses over the five-year period.
Before spending money on energy efficient equipment for your business, you can implement simple tips and
strategies that can easily cut costs by 20%. About 30% of the energy used in commercial and industrial buildings is
used inefficiently or unnecessarily. If the energy efficiency of our commercial and industrial buildings was improved
by 10%, the reduction in greenhouse gas emissions would be the equivalent of taking 30 million cars off the road.
The largest energy consumer in office buildings is generally the heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVaC),
as well as lighting. Here’s how you can save energy:
• Switch off: Involve staff and increase awareness about the need to switch lights off and turn appliances off
when not in use. Make this part of the company culture. Remind staff at meetings and place stickers above
light switches. Turn appliances off at the wall plug, rather than leaving them on standby as this can still draw
about 20% or more of normal electricity use (examples are TVs, music systems, computers, phone chargers
etc). Also turn the geyser off when you go on holiday.
A photocopy machine left on overnight wastes enough electricity to print 16 000 copies.
• Use only the light you need: Light switches should be clearly labelled to help employees to select only those
lights they need (for example, when cleaning or restocking the store after hours). Lights in unoccupied areas
should be switched off.
Occupancy or daylight sensors are also useful – this way you don’t need to rely on people to remember to
switch off. Sensors can achieve savings of up to 50% on lighting costs in some operations.
© 2024 Fetola (Pty) Ltd • Phone: 0861111690 • sabfoundationtholoana@fetola.co.za 55

• Maintenance: Failing lamps or equipment should be reported by staff and replaced or repaired. This will
help maintain optimum equipment efficiency, and in turn, provide a safer working environment.
Keep windows and light fittings clean, replace old, dim lamps with new, low-energy LED technology, keep
controls and timers in good working order, and repair any faulty equipment. We tend to maintain or service
equipment only when things go wrong. A regular, documented cleaning and maintenance schedule will keep
your equipment running longer and more efficiently. For example, a fridge compressor that never stops running
due to a faulty thermostat can use double the electricity – all unnecessary and avoidable costs.
• Heating and cooling: Insulate the ceiling/roof. This can keep a home or business premises up to 5 degrees
(Celsius) warmer in winter, and 10 degrees cooler in summer (and save on heating and cooling costs). Savings
of about 75% can be achieved by adding a ceiling and insulation, or 25% for just adding insulation (if there
is already a ceiling).
Reduce excessive heating or cooling. Centralised heating or cooling is a big power guzzler.
Use localised equipment and only heat or cool occupied rooms. The room temperature should not be more than
10 degrees (Celsius) more or less than the outside/ ambient temperature. Fan or oil heaters with thermostats
are best.
• Energy efficient bulbs: Use compact-fluorescent (CFL) or LED lights, which can easily replace standard bulbs
in most fixtures. Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFLs) use 75% less power than old incandescent bulbs and last
much longer. (Note that CFLs contain small amounts of harmful chemicals, so don’t throw them in the bin. Please
dispose of them safely. Take them to safe ‘drop off’ points at retailers like Woolworths or Pick n Pay instead.
Though CFL and LED lights both have a higher purchase price than standard incandescent bulbs, they last
significantly longer and use less energy.
Water savings
There’s no substitute for water! We are in the midst of one of the worst droughts in 30 years and climate-forecasting
models predict that rainfall is going to become increasingly variable and dry. In other words, if we apply business
terminology, South Africa could be facing “a water recession”. To make matters worse, water quality is not always
reliable. Most of South Africa’s 800 municipal water treatment plants are faulty and not working at maximum
capacity.
How can we take responsibility in our own area of business activity?
• Flush sensibly: We flush large amounts of drinking water down the toilet daily. However, with good
maintenance and simple water-saving initiatives, toilet water consumption can be significantly reduced. Older
toilet cisterns with a syphon flushing system hold 9–12ℓ of water. Modern toilet cisterns hold about 6ℓ of water.
Converting your toilet to a multi-flush system, which flushes for as long as the handle is held down, or a dual-
flush system, which offers long and short flush options, can cut your water bill by up to 20%.
• Taps and basins: Tap aerators and flow-restrictors can be fitted onto bathroom and kitchen taps, reducing
normal tap flow from 20–30ℓ per minute, much of which is wasted, to a more sensible 6–10ℓ. These water-
efficient devices channel water through a small screen that is screwed onto tap openings. This can reduce the
water flow from your tap by 50–75%, while the water pressure remains the same.
• Find your leaks and fix them
Green is good - Plant a tree!
Trees provide all sorts of valuable services. Trees are important climate regulators, they provide shade and prevent
extreme surface water run-off. By planting a tree, we do others a favour too – providing habitat for birds and
insects, play swings for children and a resource for future generations. So, do the world a favour and plant a tree.

16. Small Business Compliance Basic Information Sheet
PAYE
• Every employer (person, partnership or company who employs at least one person) that has an employee that
earns above R7 980.00 per month, is required to apply to SARS, for registration as an employer within 21
business days of becoming an employer in order to pay Employee’s Tax.
• One-person businesses with no employees do not have to register for PAYE. That individual would be classified
as a provisional taxpayer.
• Employee’s Tax is the tax required to be deducted or withheld by the employer from the remuneration paid/
payable to an employee as it is earned by the employee, this is known as Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE).
• The PAYE amount withheld from the employees must be declared Monthly Employer Declaration Form (EMP201)
and paid over to SARS on or before the 7th of the following month.
• Employers must provide employees with an IRP5 form which discloses the tax deducted from their salaries.
• An employer who is required to register with SARS for PAYE is also required to register for Unemployment
Insurance Fund (UIF) contributions (more detail is provided below).

Unemployment Insurance Fund
• You are required by law to register with the Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) and contribute towards the
fund if you have employed people who work for you for more than 24 hours per month.
• One-person business with no employees does not have to register for UIF.
• It is the employer’s responsibility to register each of their employees for UIF, unless an employee is one of the following:
– An employee working less than 24 hours per month for a single employer.
– Foreigners working on contract; or
– Workers who only earn commission
• Employers must pay UIF contributions of 2% of the value of each worker’s monthly remuneration: 1% is
deducted from the employee’s salary and 1% is contributed by the employer.
• The payment must be made to the UIF or SARS before the 7th of every month (for the previous month’s contributions).
• Employees are entitled to claim benefits from the Fund at the Department of
• Labour in the event that they become unemployed for reasons beyond their control. This includes the payment
of illness, maternity, adoption and dependant’s benefits related to the unemployment of those employees (if
benefits are not being received from the Compensation fund in this regard).
• When an employee gets fired or goes on maternity leave the employee can then claim UIF benefits at the
department of labour.

Skills Development Levy
• If an employer believes that in the following 12-month period the remuneration payable to employees will
exceed R500 000, the employer must register for the Skills Development Levy.
• Those required to register for the Skills development Fund must register through SARS and indicate the
jurisdiction of the SETA (Skills Education and Training Authority) within which the employer must be classified
as well as the applicable SIC code.
• Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) Codes are updated each year.
• One-person businesses with no employees do not have to register for the SDL.
• The Skills Development Levy constitutes a payment of 1% of the employee’s salary and is paid by the employer
over to SARS with the EMP201 submission.
• Up to 20% of the amount paid in respect of the Levy can be claimed back upon the successful submission of
a Workplace Skills Plan (WSP) to your relevant SETA by 30th April each year for training to be conducted in
the year to come.
• An Annual Training Report (ATR) must also be submitted to the SETA latest 30th April for training conducted
over the past year.
• The following employers are exempt from paying the Skills Development Levy:
– The public service employer
– A public benefit organisation that is in possession of an exemption letter issued by SARS
– Public entities that get more than 80% of their money from Parliament
– Employers who do not have to register according to the Income Tax Act

BEE Compliance
Exempt Micro Enterprise (EME)- Affidavit:
• Companies with a turnover of less than R10 million annually qualify as an Exempt Micro Enterprise (EME) and
are automatically assigned a good BEE score (minimum BEE level 4).
• For a business with a turnover of less than R10 million, a B-BBEE certificate is not required. An Affidavit can be
completed and signed by a Commissioner of Oaths, instead of the B-BBEE certificate. Once the Affidavit has
been stamped by a Commissioner of Oaths, the Affidavit serves as a BEE certificate as no other verification is
required for an Exempted Micro Enterprise.
• The affidavit is valid for 12 months.
• an EME is allowed to be measured in terms of the Qualifying Small Enterprise scorecard if it wishes to maximise
its points and move to a higher B-BBEE score.
Visit the CIPC website to download your affidavit.
Qualifying Small Enterprise (QSE)
• If a company has annual revenue of between R10 million and R50 million qualifies as a Qualifying Small
Enterprise.
• Qualifying Small Enterprises which 100% black ownership are deemed to be B-BBEE Level 1, with 135%
procurement recognition. A sworn affidavit can be completed.
• QSEs with over 51% black ownership qualify for B-BBEE Level 2, with a 125% procurement rating. A sworn
affidavit can be completed.
• QSEs with black ownership less than 51% need to be measured according to their industry codes. Thus, a
SANAS (South African National Accredited System) accredited B-BBEE verification needs to be done.
VAT Registration
• An entity with a turnover of more than R1 million in any 12-month consecutive period is liable for compulsory
VAT registration.
• An entity with a turnover greater than R50 000 but less than R1 million can register voluntarily as a VAT vendor.
• Entities must complete and submit VAT101e forms to register; these are available from the SARS website or
from any SARS branch.
• VAT must be charged at 15% for all services and/or products supplied by the vendor.
• Input VAT, VAT that can be claimed back from SARS, can only be claimed on goods to be used in the course of
making supplies/supplying a service. i.e. not for items that are wholly consumed during personal use.
