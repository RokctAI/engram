For this reason, identifying your core values needs to be a two-step process. Ideally this is done as a team, so that the organisation is aligned in their thinking:

*   **Step One** – Choose a value or belief that you feel is important in your business. Examples include ethics, trust, transparency, fairness, innovation, quality, service, reliability etc.
*   **Step Two** – Write down how you will (or do) turn this value or belief into action.

This second step is very important, as action is more important than words. Remember that the behaviour needs to extend throughout the organisation, not just in the leadership.

#### Example:
*   **Value** = "Quality"
*   **Problem** = How to make Quality happen all through the business?
*   **Action** = Set quality standards in all areas of the business, implement quality management systems, measure customer complaints, provide quality training to each staff member, etc.

Follow this process in your own time to determine your own set of company Values, and how you will turn them into reality through action.

### Ethics in Leadership

> Former Chief Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court Potter Stewart stated: *"Ethics is knowing the difference between what you have the right to do, and what is right to do."*

All companies stand to profit from a reputation for acting with honesty and integrity. Ethical behaviour in business can ensure long-term sustainability and respectability.

As a business leader, your behaviour demonstrates the ethical beliefs and practices of your company. It can directly influence the values and ethics of your staff and employees, affecting everything from company culture to sales and investments.

So essentially **“walk the talk”**.

#### Some points to consider:
*   What do ethics mean to you?
*   Do ethics apply in your personal and business life? Both?
*   Are your stakeholders affected by your decisions/actions?
