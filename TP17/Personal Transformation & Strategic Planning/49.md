*   **Maintenance:** Failing lamps or equipment should be reported by staff and replaced or repaired. This will help maintain optimum equipment efficiency, and in turn, provide a safer working environment.
    *   *Keep windows and light fittings clean, replace old, dim lamps with new, low-energy LED technology, keep controls and timers in good working order, and repair any faulty equipment. We tend to maintain or service equipment only when things go wrong. A regular, documented cleaning and maintenance schedule will keep your equipment running longer and more efficiently. For example, a fridge compressor that never stops running due to a faulty thermostat can use double the electricity – all unnecessary and avoidable costs.*
*   **Heating and cooling:** Insulate the ceiling/roof. This can keep a home or business premises up to 5 degrees (Celsius) warmer in winter, and 10 degrees cooler in summer (and save on heating and cooling costs). Savings of about 75% can be achieved by adding a ceiling and insulation, or 25% for just adding insulation (if there is already a ceiling).
    *   *Reduce excessive heating or cooling. Centralised heating or cooling is a big power guzzler. Use localised equipment and only heat or cool occupied rooms. The room temperature should not be more than 10 degrees (Celsius) more or less than the outside/ ambient temperature. Fan or oil heaters with thermostats are best.*
*   **Energy efficient bulbs:** Use compact-fluorescent (CFL) or LED lights, which can easily replace standard bulbs in most fixtures. Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFLs) use 75% less power than old incandescent bulbs and last much longer. *(Note that CFLs contain small amounts of harmful chemicals, so don’t throw them in the bin. Please dispose of them safely. Take them to safe ‘drop off’ points at retailers like Woolworths or Pick n Pay instead. Though CFL and LED lights both have a higher purchase price than standard incandescent bulbs, they last significantly longer and use less energy.)*

### Water savings

There’s no substitute for water! We are in the midst of one of the worst droughts in 30 years and climate-forecasting models predict that rainfall is going to become increasingly variable and dry. In other words, if we apply business terminology, South Africa could be facing “a water recession”. To make matters worse, water quality is not always reliable. Most of South Africa’s 800 municipal water treatment plants are faulty and not working at maximum capacity.

How can we take responsibility in our own area of business activity?

*   **Flush sensibly:** We flush large amounts of drinking water down the toilet daily. However, with good maintenance and simple water-saving initiatives, toilet water consumption can be significantly reduced. Older toilet cisterns with a syphon flushing system hold 9–12ℓ of water. Modern toilet cisterns hold about 6ℓ of water. Converting your toilet to a multi-flush system, which flushes for as long as the handle is held down, or a dual-flush system, which offers long and short flush options, can cut your water bill by up to 20%.
*   **Taps and basins:** Tap aerators and flow-restrictors can be fitted onto bathroom and kitchen taps, reducing normal tap flow from 20–30ℓ per minute, much of which is wasted, to a more sensible 6–10ℓ. These water-efficient devices channel water through a small screen that is screwed onto tap openings. This can reduce the water flow from your tap by 50–75%, while the water pressure remains the same.

### Green is good - Plant a tree!

Trees provide all sorts of valuable services. Trees are important climate regulators, they provide shade and prevent extreme surface water run-off. By planting a tree, we do others a favour too – providing habitat for birds and insects, play swings for children and a resource for future generations. So, do the world a favour and plant a tree.
